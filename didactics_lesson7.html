Section 1 · Foundations

The Humanistic Revolution in Language Teaching

🧘
Beyond Drills — The Whole Person Approach
In the 1970s-80s, educators began questioning ALM's mechanical drills. New methods emerged focusing on the WHOLE LEARNER — emotions, autonomy, and stress-free learning environments.

This lesson covers four alternative/humanistic methods that challenged traditional approaches. Each addresses different aspects of the learning experience.

🤫
Silent Way
Gattegno · Learner autonomy

Focus: Students discover language rules independently. Teacher stays mostly SILENT.

🎵
Suggestopedia
Lozanov · Music & relaxation

Focus: Reduce anxiety through music, comfortable environment, and positive suggestion.

🏃
TPR
Asher · Physical actions

Focus: Link language to physical movement. Comprehension before production.

👥
CLL
Curran · Counselor approach

Focus: Teacher as counselor, building trust and emotional safety in a learning community.

What These Methods Share

Learner-centered — Focus on student needs, not just content
Affective factors — Reduce anxiety, build confidence
Autonomy — Students take responsibility for learning
Humanistic psychology — Influenced by Rogers, Maslow
⚠️Exam Tip: Know which FOUNDER goes with which METHOD. This is a very common exam question!
Section 2 · The Silent Way

Silent Way — Teacher Silent, Students Discover

🤫
Learning Through Discovery, Not Imitation
Caleb Gattegno developed Silent Way based on the idea that language is NOT learned through imitation or habit formation, but through COGNITIVE DISCOVERY of rules.

Core Principles of Silent Way

Founder: Caleb GATTEGNO
Theory: Cognitive — learners DISCOVER rules (not habit formation)
Teacher role: Mostly SILENT — a facilitator who gives minimal clues
Student role: Active problem-solvers discovering language patterns
Error correction: Self-correction through teacher's silent cues

Silent Way Materials

Cuisenaire Rods — Colored rods of different lengths to represent words, syllables, or grammar structures
Sound-Color Charts (Fidel) — Charts linking sounds to colors for pronunciation
Word Charts — Vocabulary displayed on wall charts
Pointer — Teacher points silently to guide students

Silent Way Techniques

1. Teacher silence — Say once, then stay silent; students figure it out
2. Self-correction — Teacher gestures or points to help students find errors
3. Peer correction — Students help each other
4. Rod manipulation — Using rods to build sentences visually
5. Problem-solving tasks — Students discover grammar patterns
✅ Strengths

Promotes learner autonomy • Develops problem-solving • Reduces teacher-dependency • Encourages critical thinking • Active student engagement

❌ Limitations

Confusing for beginners • Requires motivated learners • Teacher training needed • Slow progress • Limited to certain contexts

💡Memory Tip: Silent Way = GATTEGNO + RODS + SILENCE + DISCOVERY. Teacher speaks minimally; students discover rules.
Section 3 · Suggestopedia

Suggestopedia — Music, Relaxation & Suggestion

🎵
Unlocking the Mind Through Positive Suggestion
Georgi Lozanov, a Bulgarian psychiatrist, believed that psychological barriers block learning. Suggestopedia uses music, relaxation, and positive suggestion to "de-suggest" these barriers.

Core Principles of Suggestopedia

Founder: Georgi LOZANOV (Bulgaria)
Theory: Learning is blocked by psychological barriers; positive suggestion removes them
Environment: Comfortable, decorated classroom; soft lighting; music
Goal: Accelerated learning through relaxation and lowered affective filter
Key concept: "Desuggestopedia" — removing mental blocks

Key Elements of Suggestopedia

Authority — Teacher as trusted, confident authority figure
Infantilization — Students adopt new "identities" (names); playful atmosphere
Double-planedness — Conscious (linguistic) + subconscious (music/environment) learning
Intonation/Rhythm — Teacher reads with dramatic variation
Concert Reading — Two "concerts" with music accompaniment

Suggestopedia Techniques

1. First Concert (Active) — Teacher reads dialogue dramatically with classical music; students follow text
2. Second Concert (Passive) — Teacher reads calmly with baroque music; students relax, eyes closed
3. Peripheral Learning — Grammar posters on walls absorbed subconsciously
4. Role-play with new identities — Students adopt foreign names/personas
5. Positive suggestion — "You will learn easily and remember everything"
✅ Strengths

Reduces anxiety • Creates positive attitude • Memorable experiences • Engages whole brain • Can accelerate learning

❌ Limitations

Resource-intensive (music, decor) • Limited empirical evidence • Neglects grammar accuracy • Cultural concerns • Teacher training expensive

⚠️Exam Trap: Remember the TWO CONCERTS: First = Active (classical music, follow text), Second = Passive (baroque music, relax). Also: PERIPHERAL learning = posters on walls.
Section 4 · Total Physical Response

TPR — Commands & Physical Actions

🏃
Language Through Movement
James Asher observed that children understand language before they speak. TPR mimics this by having students respond to commands with PHYSICAL ACTIONS before speaking.

Core Principles of TPR

Founder: James ASHER
Theory: Comprehension BEFORE production (like child L1 acquisition)
Main technique: IMPERATIVE commands + physical responses
Skill sequence: Listening → Understanding → Speaking (delayed)
Stress-free: No forced speaking; students act silently at first

TPR Techniques

Imperative commands — "Stand up!" "Open the door!" "Touch your nose!"
Action sequences — Complex chains: "Walk to the door, open it, and wave."
Teacher modeling — Teacher demonstrates first, students follow
Student-to-student commands — Later, students give commands to each other
Role reversal — Students eventually become "teachers"

TPR Progression Stages

Stage 1: Students listen and respond physically (no speaking)
Stage 2: Students begin responding verbally when ready
Stage 3: Students give commands to others
Stage 4: More complex language introduced (reading, writing)
✅ Strengths

Highly engaging • Stress-free for beginners • Great for kinesthetic learners • Memorable through action • Good for young learners

❌ Limitations

Limited vocabulary range • Not effective for abstract concepts • Less useful for advanced learners • Mostly for beginners • Classroom management challenges

💡Memory Tip: TPR = ASHER + COMMANDS + ACTIONS. Comprehension FIRST, production LATER. Perfect for BEGINNERS and KINESTHETIC learners.
Section 5 · Community Language Learning

CLL — Teacher as Counselor

👥
Learning in a Supportive Community
Charles Curran adapted counseling techniques to language learning. The teacher acts as a COUNSELOR supporting "clients" (students) through emotional challenges of learning.

Core Principles of CLL

Founder: Charles A. CURRAN (based on Carl Rogers)
Theory: Counseling-Learning — address emotional needs of learners
Teacher role: COUNSELOR — supportive, non-judgmental, available
Student role: "Clients" — vulnerable learners building confidence
L1 use: ALLOWED — students speak in L1, teacher translates to L2

CLL Typical Process

1. Students sit in circle — community feeling
2. Student says message in L1 — what they want to communicate
3. Teacher whispers L2 translation — stands behind student
4. Student repeats in L2 — recorded on tape
5. Class listens to recording — analyzes and discusses language
6. Reflection session — discuss feelings about the experience

CLL Techniques

Recording — Student conversations recorded and transcribed
Transcription analysis — Class studies the recorded language
Reflection on experience — Students share feelings about learning
Human computer — Teacher repeats phrases as many times as requested
Small group tasks — Building community and trust
✅ Strengths

Reduces anxiety • Builds community • Learner-driven content • Addresses emotional needs • Personalized learning

❌ Limitations

Very time-consuming • No structured syllabus • Requires small groups • Teacher must know L1 • May not fit curricula

⚠️Exam Trap: CLL is the ONLY method here that explicitly USES L1. Teacher = COUNSELOR (Curran). Don't confuse with Silent Way where teacher is SILENT (Gattegno).

Section 7 · Listening Comprehension

🎙️ Listen — A Trainer Explains Alternative Methods

Press ▶ to listen to a teacher trainer describing the four methods. Identify which method she is describing in each part.

A Trainer Compares Alternative Methods
~60 seconds · British English

Trainer: "Let me explain four alternative teaching methods. The first one was developed by Gattegno. The teacher stays mostly silent while students discover language rules on their own. They use colored rods to build sentences and learn through problem-solving rather than imitation."

"The second method was created by a Bulgarian psychiatrist named Lozanov. It uses classical and baroque music to create a relaxed atmosphere. The teacher reads dialogues dramatically while students follow along, then reads again softly while students relax with eyes closed. Posters on the walls help students learn subconsciously."

"The third method, developed by James Asher, is based on how children learn their first language — understanding comes before speaking. The teacher gives commands like 'stand up' or 'touch your nose,' and students respond with physical actions. Speaking is delayed until students are ready."

"The fourth method treats the teacher as a counselor supporting vulnerable learners. Students sit in a circle and express what they want to say in their native language. The teacher whispers the translation, and students repeat it. The conversation is recorded and analyzed later. This method was developed by Curran."

Which method is the trainer describing? — Match each statement

"The teacher stays mostly silent while students discover language rules on their own. They use colored rods." Which method?
"Uses classical and baroque music. Teacher reads dialogues dramatically, then reads softly while students relax." Which method?
"Teacher gives commands like 'stand up' or 'touch your nose,' and students respond with physical actions." Which method?
"Treats the teacher as a counselor. Students express what they want to say in their native language, teacher whispers the translation." Which method?

Section 8 · Interview Preparation

Interview Practice — Explain a Method

ℹ️Note: The written exam is 100% multiple choice (A, B, C, D, E). However, in the oral interview, you may be asked to explain a method. This section prepares you for that.

Practice explaining Total Physical Response (TPR) in 60–80 words. Include founder, main principle, and techniques.

✍️Structure your answer:
1. Name the method and founder (Asher)
2. Main principle (comprehension before production)
3. How it works (commands → physical actions)
4. Best suited for (beginners, kinesthetic learners)
5. One limitation
0 words (aim for 60–80)
✅ Good practice!

Model Answer:
"Total Physical Response, or TPR, was developed by James Asher. The main principle is that comprehension should come before production, just like how children learn their first language. The teacher gives imperative commands such as 'stand up' or 'open the door,' and students respond with physical actions without speaking. Speaking is delayed until students feel ready. This method is particularly effective for beginners and kinesthetic learners who learn through movement. However, it's limited to basic vocabulary and concrete actions, making it less suitable for advanced learners or abstract concepts."
