Section 1 · Core Definitions

Language, Learning & Teaching

📖
The Foundation of Everything
Start here! These are the most fundamental terms you need to know.
Language▼
A rule-governed system of sounds and words used to communicate ideas and feelings.
Communication▼
The process of sending and receiving information between people.
Learning▼
A relatively permanent change in behaviour based on experiences or discoveries.
Teaching▼
Giving someone knowledge, showing how to do something, or changing their ideas through instruction.
Skill▼
An ability that comes from experience, training, or practice.
4 main skills: Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing
Sub-skill▼
A component ability within each main skill.
Examples: Skimming, scanning, fluency, editing
Grammar▼
The structure and system of a language (syntax + morphology).
Remember: M.F.U.P = Meaning, Form, Use, Pronunciation
Tense▼
The expression of location in time (past, present, future).
Aspect▼
The expression of duration/structure of an action (simple, progressive, perfect).
Section 2 · Teaching Methodology

Approach, Method & Technique

👨‍🏫
The Hierarchy of Teaching
Understand the difference: Approach → Method → Technique → Procedure
💡Memory Aid: Approach (theory) → Method (system) → Technique (activity) → Procedure (steps)
Approach▼
The theory/philosophy behind teaching — beliefs about how language is learned.
Example: Communicative Approach = language is for communication
Method▼
A systematic set of teaching procedures based on an approach.
Examples: GTM, ALM, Direct Method, CLT
Technique▼
A specific classroom activity used to achieve a learning goal.
Examples: Role-play, drilling, elicitation, miming
Procedure▼
Step-by-step instructions for applying a method in class.
Example: PPP stages = Presentation → Practice → Production
Learner-Centredness▼
Student is the center of learning; teacher is facilitator/monitor.
Contrast: Teacher-centered = teacher controls everything
Humanistic Approach▼
Involves learners at all levels, focusing on their whole personality, values, and emotions.
Methods: Silent Way, CLL, Suggestopedia, TPR
Exercise▼
A way of practicing what was presented (fill blanks, matching, rewrite).
Task▼
Activity with a real-world outcome; students discover patterns themselves.
Example: "Create an action plan for positive changes in your school"
Section 3 · Classroom Techniques

Teaching Techniques

🎯
What Teachers Actually DO in Class
Specific activities, techniques, and classroom practices.
Drills▼
Repetitive exercises to practice a language item.
Types: Repetition, substitution, transformation drills
Elicitation▼
Drawing information out of learners through guided questions instead of giving answers directly.
Based on: Discovery approach / Constructivism
Miming▼
Using gestures and body movements (no words) to show meaning.
Warm-up▼
Fun activities at the beginning of a lesson to engage students and set context.
Review▼
Activities at the end of a session to reinforce key concepts previously taught.
Recycling▼
Reviewing previously taught vocabulary/skills in meaningful activities in later lessons.
Micro-teaching▼
Reduced teaching situation (shorter duration, smaller class) used in teacher training.
Example: Teach "body parts" vocabulary in 10 minutes to 5 students
Brainstorming▼
Generating ideas quickly without judging quality — used before writing.
Information Gap▼
Activity where students have different information and must communicate to complete a task.
Information Transfer▼
Moving information from one format to another.
Example: Complete a table using information from a map
Jigsaw Activity▼
Students find different information about a topic, then share with their group.
Promotes: Peer-teaching, collaboration, communication
Jigsaw Reading▼
Re-ordering a mixed-up text to find correct order — promotes collaborative learning.
Cloze▼
Text with words removed and replaced by gaps for students to fill.
Recognition Exercises▼
Students recognize correct answers from options (multiple choice, matching).
Productive Exercises▼
Students produce their own answers (no options given).
Section 4 · Teacher Roles & Classroom

Teacher Roles & Classroom Management

🏫
Managing the Learning Environment
Teacher roles, checking understanding, interaction patterns.
Facilitator▼
A teacher who guides learners to find their own answers rather than providing them.
Key role in: CLT (Communicative Language Teaching)
Monitoring▼
Walking around the class during activities to check progress, help students, and note errors.
Feedback▼
Comments about learner performance — can be written or oral.
Praise▼
Positive feedback that reinforces good behaviour and increases motivation.
Example: "Well done!" "Excellent work!"
CCQ (Concept Checking Question)▼
Questions to check if students understood the MEANING of new language.
Example: "Is this past or present?" "Can you use this with friends?"
ICQ (Instruction Checking Question)▼
Questions to check if students understood the INSTRUCTIONS.
Example: "Do you work alone or in pairs?" "How many sentences?"
Interaction▼
Patterns of communication — verbal or non-verbal.
Patterns: T-S, S-S, Ss-Ss, mingle, open pairs
Group Dynamic▼
The way a group of people interact with each other.
Mixed-Ability Class▼
A class with learners at different proficiency levels (beginners, intermediate, advanced).
Body Language▼
Non-verbal communication: eye contact, facial expressions, gestures, posture.
Intonation▼
Changes in pitch and stress to express emotions or attitudes when speaking.
Checklist▼
A list identifying areas students should focus on when correcting mistakes.
Section 5 · Language Skills

Reading, Writing & Sub-skills

📚
The Four Skills & Processing Strategies
Sub-skills for reading, writing, listening, and speaking.
Integrated Skills▼
Combining different language skills in one activity.
Example: Listen to a dialogue, then write a summary
Accuracy▼
Producing language correctly (grammar, vocabulary, pronunciation).
Fluency▼
Producing language easily and quickly, even if not perfectly correct.
Skimming▼
Quick reading to get the main idea (gist).
Scanning▼
Reading to find specific information.
Intensive Reading▼
Careful, detailed reading of a short text for specific learning aims.
Extensive Reading▼
Reading longer texts for enjoyment and general skill development.
Predicting▼
Guessing what a text will be about before reading.
Previewing/Surveying▼
Looking at pictures, titles, subtitles to get the general idea before reading.
Inferring▼
Guessing meaning from context — "reading between the lines."
Top-Down Processing▼
Using prior knowledge (schemata) to understand new texts.
Direction: Big picture → details
Bottom-Up Processing▼
Building meaning from smallest units (letters, words, sentences).
Direction: Details → big picture
SQ4R▼
Reading strategy: Survey, Question, Read, Recite, Review, Reflect.
Editing▼
Correcting spelling, punctuation, and grammar in writing. Can be self or peer.
Section 6 · Vocabulary & Materials

Vocabulary, Functions & Materials

📝
Language Components & Teaching Resources
Vocabulary types, language functions, and classroom materials.
Functions▼
Fixed phrases for communicative purposes (speech acts).
Examples: Requesting, suggesting, apologizing, asking for advice
Notions▼
General concepts expressed through language.
Examples: Time, distance, space, quality
Chunks▼
Fixed or semi-fixed expressions learned and used as whole units.
Examples: "I'm fine," "pretty good," "right away," collocations
Lexical Items▼
A word or group of related words (word family).
Example: Furniture: chair, table, sofa, desk
Contextualization▼
Teaching vocabulary in a meaningful context rather than in isolation.
Circumlocution▼
Using many words to explain an idea when you don't know the exact word.
Strategic competence: A communication strategy
Metalanguage▼
Language used to talk about language.
Examples: "Noun," "verb," "past tense" — grammar terminology
Proverbs & Idioms▼
Fixed expressions understood as a whole. Proverbs teach moral lessons.
Authentic Materials▼
Materials originally created for real-life use, not for teaching.
Examples: Newspapers, menus, podcasts, TV shows
Realia▼
Real objects used to present vocabulary.
Examples: Fruits, clothes, pens, toys
Visual Aids▼
Tools for visual learners: pictures, flashcards, graphs, videos.
Learner Training▼
Teaching learners HOW to learn: recording vocabulary, continuing outside class.
Learning Strategies▼
How learners cope with problems, accumulate new rules, and automate existing ones.
Section 7 · Learning Theories & SLA

Cognitive & SLA Theories

🧠
How Learning Happens
Key theorists, hypotheses, and cognitive concepts.
💡Memory Aid: KRASHEN = Input (i+1) | SWAIN = Output | LONG = Interaction | SCHMIDT = Noticing | VYGOTSKY = ZPD
Input (i+1)▼
Language slightly above current level. i = current; +1 = next level.
Krashen: Comprehensible input drives acquisition
Intake▼
The portion of input that learners actually process and store (internalize).
Remember: Input = all exposure; Intake = what's actually learned
Noticing▼
Consciously paying attention to form, use, and meaning of new language.
Schmidt: You can't learn what you don't notice
Consciousness-Raising▼
Explicit instruction to make learners aware of language rules.
Schemata▼
Learner's existing background knowledge. "Pre-existent knowledge of the world" (Cook).
Schemes▼
Innate cognitive structures that enable interaction with the world (Piaget).
Example: Babies have schemes for biting, sucking, grasping
Assimilation▼
Understanding new experiences using existing schemes (Piaget).
Example: Baby uses "sucking" scheme on new objects
Accommodation▼
Modifying existing schemes to fit new situations (Piaget).
Example: Baby learns not to throw glass after it breaks
Equilibration▼
Restoring balance between current understanding and new experiences (Piaget).
Adaptation▼
Adjusting schemes through assimilation and accommodation (Piaget).
Scaffolding▼
Support and guidance teachers provide to help learners accomplish tasks they couldn't do alone.
ZPD (Zone of Proximal Development)▼
Gap between what learners can do alone and what they can do with help (Vygotsky).
Tasks should: Be slightly above current level but achievable with support
Reinforcer▼
A consequence that maintains or INCREASES a behaviour.
Negative Reinforcement▼
Removing something unpleasant to INCREASE a behaviour.
⚠️ Trap: It INCREASES behaviour — it's NOT punishment!
Presentation Punishment▼
Adding something unpleasant to DECREASE a behaviour.
Removal Punishment▼
Taking away something pleasant to DECREASE a behaviour.
⚠️Exam Trap: i+1 = KRASHEN | ZPD = VYGOTSKY — Similar concepts, different theorists! Don't confuse them!
Section 8 · Assessment & Competence

Assessment & Communicative Competence

📊
Testing & Competence Models
How we measure learning and what it means to "know" a language.
Testing Tools▼
Means of assessment: quizzes, cloze tests, multiple choice, presentations, portfolios.
Item▼
A single question or element in a test.
Standard▼
Both a goal and a unit for measuring progress toward that goal.
Norm-Referenced Test▼
Performance compared to OTHER test-takers.
Examples: Teaching exam (concours), master's entrance exam
Criterion-Referenced Test▼
Test with a specified passing criterion (e.g., 10/20).
Example: Baccalaureate exam — pass/fail based on fixed score
Holistic Scoring▼
ONE overall score for the whole work based on general impression.
Analytic Scoring▼
Separate scores for different criteria (grammar, spelling, content, etc.).
Rubrics▼
The criteria upon which student performance is graded.
Competence▼
The system of rules representing a learner's knowledge of language (Chomsky).
vs Performance: Competence = knowledge; Performance = actual use
Competency▼
A set of skills, abilities, and knowledge used to accomplish a specific task.
Communicative Competence▼
Knowing what to say, to whom, where, when, and how (Hymes).
Canale & Swain: 4 components below
Grammatical Competence▼
Knowledge of grammar, vocabulary, phonology, and semantics.
Sociolinguistic Competence▼
Using language appropriately in social contexts (register, politeness).
Example: Using formal language with boss, informal with friends
Discourse Competence▼
Connecting sentences coherently (cohesion, coherence).
Example: Using "however," "therefore" to connect ideas
Strategic Competence▼
Strategies to overcome communication breakdowns.
Examples: Paraphrasing, circumlocution, "I mean," "mm"
⚠️Exam Traps: NORM-REFERENCED = compare to others | CRITERION-REFERENCED = fixed standard | HOLISTIC = one score | ANALYTIC = multiple scores | HYMES coined Communicative Competence | CANALE & SWAIN = 4 components
