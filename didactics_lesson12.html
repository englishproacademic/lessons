Section 1 · Foundations

What is SLA?

🌍
Understanding How People Learn a Second Language
Second Language Acquisition (SLA) is the scientific study of how people learn languages other than their native language. It draws on linguistics, psychology, and education.

SLA — Key Definitions

SLA: Second Language Acquisition — the study of how people learn additional languages
L1: First Language (native language / mother tongue)
L2: Second Language (any language learned after L1)
Target Language: The language being learned

Key Questions SLA Addresses

• How do people acquire a second language?
• What role does input play? What about output?
• Is there an optimal age for language learning?
• What factors affect success in L2 learning?
💡Important Distinction: ACQUISITION is subconscious (like how children learn L1). LEARNING is conscious (studying rules). This distinction is central to Krashen's theory.
Section 2 · Age & Acquisition

Critical Period Hypothesis

👶
Is There an Optimal Age for Language Learning?
The Critical Period Hypothesis suggests that there is a window of time when language acquisition is easiest, and after this period, it becomes more difficult.

Critical Period Hypothesis (CPH)

Proposed by: Eric LENNEBERG (1967)
Definition: There is a critical period (roughly birth to puberty) during which language acquisition occurs most naturally
After puberty: The brain loses plasticity, making native-like proficiency harder
Evidence: Children often achieve better pronunciation and implicit grammar
AspectYounger LearnersOlder Learners
PronunciationOften achieve native-like accentMore likely to retain L1 accent
Grammar intuitionDevelop implicit knowledgeRely more on explicit rules
Learning speedSlower initiallyFaster initial progress
Ultimate attainmentHigher ceilingMay plateau earlier

Related Concepts

Sensitive Period: A weaker version — learning is easier but not impossible afterward
Brain Lateralization: Language functions become localized in one hemisphere as the brain matures
Ultimate Attainment: The final level of L2 proficiency a learner achieves
⚠️Exam Tip: CPH was proposed by LENNEBERG (1967). Native-like attainment is harder AFTER PUBERTY. Critical Period ≠ impossible after puberty, just harder.
Section 3 · Input-Based Theory

Krashen's 5 Hypotheses

📚
The Most Influential SLA Theory
Stephen Krashen proposed five interconnected hypotheses explaining how second languages are acquired. His theory emphasizes COMPREHENSIBLE INPUT.
1️⃣
Acquisition-Learning Hypothesis
Two distinct ways to develop L2
Click to expand

ACQUISITION: Subconscious process, like how children learn L1. Results in implicit knowledge.

LEARNING: Conscious process of studying rules. Results in explicit knowledge.

Key claim: Only ACQUISITION leads to fluent, spontaneous use. Learning can only "monitor."

2️⃣
Monitor Hypothesis
Learned knowledge acts as editor
Click to expand

Definition: Learned knowledge can only be used as a MONITOR (editor) to check and correct output.

Monitor conditions: Time, focus on form, knowledge of the rule.

Types: Over-users (too careful), under-users (ignore rules), optimal users (balance).

3️⃣
Input Hypothesis (i+1)
Comprehensible input drives acquisition
Click to expand

Definition: Learners acquire language when they receive COMPREHENSIBLE INPUT slightly above their current level (i+1).

i = current level; +1 = slightly beyond current competence

Key claim: Speaking is a RESULT of acquisition, not its cause. Input is primary.

4️⃣
Affective Filter Hypothesis
Emotions can block acquisition
Click to expand

Definition: Emotional factors (anxiety, motivation, confidence) can BLOCK or ALLOW input to reach the LAD.

High filter: Anxiety, low motivation → blocks input

Low filter: Relaxed, motivated → input gets through

Implication: Create low-anxiety classrooms for better acquisition.

5️⃣
Natural Order Hypothesis
Grammar acquired in predictable order
Click to expand

Definition: Grammatical structures are acquired in a PREDICTABLE ORDER, regardless of teaching order.

Example: -ing is acquired before 3rd person -s, regardless of instruction.

Implication: Teaching order doesn't determine acquisition order.

⚠️Exam Trap: i+1 is KRASHEN's Input Hypothesis. Don't confuse with ZPD (Vygotsky) — similar concepts, different theorists! Krashen = INPUT; Swain = OUTPUT.
Section 4 · Beyond Input

Output & Interaction Hypotheses

While Krashen emphasized INPUT, other researchers argued that OUTPUT and INTERACTION are also essential for language acquisition.

Output Hypothesis — Merrill Swain (1985)

Theorist: Merrill SWAIN
Key claim: Learners need not only INPUT but also opportunities to PRODUCE language (output)
Pushed Output: When learners are pushed to produce language, they notice gaps in their knowledge

Functions of Output

1. Noticing function: Producing output helps learners notice what they DON'T know
2. Hypothesis testing: Learners test their hypotheses about the language
3. Metalinguistic function: Output promotes reflection on language
ℹ️Key insight: Speaking isn't just a result of acquisition — it actively CONTRIBUTES to acquisition.

Interaction Hypothesis — Michael Long (1983, 1996)

Theorist: Michael LONG
Key claim: Language develops through INTERACTION and NEGOTIATION OF MEANING
Negotiation of Meaning: When communication breaks down, speakers modify input

Types of Interactional Modifications

Clarification requests: "What do you mean?" "Sorry?"
Confirmation checks: "You mean you went yesterday?"
Comprehension checks: "Do you understand?" "Is that clear?"
ℹ️Key insight: Interaction provides MODIFIED INPUT tailored to the learner's level.

InputKrashenReceiving languagei+1 comprehensible input drives acquisition
OutputSwainProducing languagePushed output helps notice gaps
InteractionLongCommunicatingNegotiation of meaning provides ideal input
⚠️Exam Trap: KRASHEN = Input (i+1). SWAIN = Output. LONG = Interaction. Know the theorist-hypothesis pairs!
Section 5 · Attention & Awareness

Noticing Hypothesis

👀
You Can't Learn What You Don't Notice
Richard Schmidt proposed that conscious NOTICING of input features is necessary for acquisition. Input alone isn't enough — you must pay attention to it.

Noticing Hypothesis — Richard Schmidt (1990)

Theorist: Richard SCHMIDT
Key claim: Learners must consciously NOTICE features of input to acquire them
Input → Intake: Only NOTICED input becomes intake (what's actually processed)
Example: A learner uses past tense correctly only after consciously noticing "-ed"

Key Concepts

Input: All the language a learner is exposed to
Intake: The portion of input that is actually processed and stored
Noticing: Consciously paying attention to specific features
Noticing the gap: Realizing difference between your output and target forms

Noticing vs Consciousness Raising

Noticing (Schmidt): Paying attention to forms IN INPUT naturally
Consciousness Raising: Explicit instruction to make learners aware of rules
Both help learners pay attention to language features
💡Teaching Implication: Use input enhancement (highlighting, bold) to help learners NOTICE target features.
Section 6 · What Does It Mean to "Know" a Language?

Communicative Competence

Knowing a language is more than just knowing grammar. Communicative Competence is the ability to use language appropriately in real social contexts.

Origins

Dell Hymes (1972): Coined "Communicative Competence" — knowing WHAT to say, TO WHOM, WHERE, WHEN, and HOW
Response to Chomsky: Chomsky focused on grammatical competence; Hymes added social context

Canale & Swain's Model (1980) — 4 Components

1. Grammatical Competence: Knowledge of grammar, vocabulary, pronunciation
2. Sociolinguistic Competence: Using language appropriately in social contexts
3. Discourse Competence: Connecting sentences coherently (cohesion, coherence)
4. Strategic Competence: Strategies to overcome communication breakdowns
ComponentWhat It CoversExample
GrammaticalGrammar, vocabulary, pronunciationUsing correct verb forms
SociolinguisticAppropriateness, registerUsing formal language with boss
DiscourseCohesion, coherenceUsing "however," "therefore"
StrategicCommunication strategiesParaphrasing when stuck
⚠️Exam Tip: HYMES coined Communicative Competence. CANALE & SWAIN expanded to 4 components. Strategic = repair strategies.
