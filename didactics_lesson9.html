Section 1 · Foundations

Assessment vs Testing vs Evaluation

📊
Understanding the Key Distinctions
These terms are often confused but have distinct meanings. Understanding them is essential for effective teaching and for exam success.

Key Definitions

ASSESSMENT — The process of gathering information about learner performance
• Can be formal (tests) or informal (observation, class participation)
• Ongoing; helps inform teaching decisions

TESTING — A specific TYPE of assessment using formal instruments
• Examples: multiple-choice tests, quizzes, cloze tests, essays
• Structured and standardized; produces quantifiable results

EVALUATION — Making judgments about learner achievement
• Uses assessment data to arrive at grades/marks
• Involves interpretation and decision-making
💡Simple Formula: Assessment (gather data) → Testing (one method) → Evaluation (make judgments). Assessment is BROADER than testing.
📈
Formative
Assessment FOR learning

Purpose: Improve learning DURING instruction; provides feedback to guide next steps.

📋
Summative
Assessment OF learning

Purpose: Evaluate overall achievement at the END; results in a final grade.

Section 2 · Types of Assessment

Formative vs Summative Assessment

📊
Two Fundamental Purposes of Assessment
The most important distinction in assessment is between FORMATIVE (assessment FOR learning) and SUMMATIVE (assessment OF learning).
FeatureFormativeSummative
PurposeImprove learning during instructionEvaluate achievement at the end
TimingOngoing, throughout the courseAt the end of a unit/course
FocusProcess of learningProduct/final outcome
FeedbackDetailed, immediate, guides improvementGeneral, often just a grade
StakesLow-stakes (no/little grade impact)High-stakes (affects final grade)
ExamplesQuizzes, peer feedback, observationsFinal exams, end-of-term tests
Key phrase"Assessment FOR learning""Assessment OF learning"

Formative Assessment — "Assessment FOR Learning"

Purpose: Help learners understand where they are and what to do next
Involves: Feedback, self-assessment, peer assessment, quizzes
Example: After a grammar lesson, students exchange gap-fill exercises and provide feedback to each other
Key benefit: Teachers can adjust instruction based on results

Summative Assessment — "Assessment OF Learning"

Purpose: Evaluate overall achievement; provide a final grade
Involves: Final exams, standardized tests, end-of-unit assessments
Example: The Baccalaureate exam at the end of secondary school
Provides: A "snapshot" of learner achievement at a specific point
⚠️Exam Tip: Remember: Formative = FOR learning (ongoing, feedback-focused) | Summative = OF learning (end, grade-focused). The words "for" and "of" are key!
Section 3 · Types of Tests

Types of Tests by Purpose

📝
Different Tests for Different Purposes
Tests can be classified by their PURPOSE. Know these five types and when to use each one.
🚪
Placement Test
Given at the START to place learners in appropriate levels
Click to expand

Purpose: Determine a learner's level to place them in the right class/group.

When: At the BEGINNING, before instruction starts.

Example: A new student takes a test to be placed in Beginner, Intermediate, or Advanced class.

🔍
Diagnostic Test
Identifies specific WEAKNESSES in learner knowledge
Click to expand

Purpose: Identify specific areas where learners need additional support.

When: Before or during instruction to target problem areas.

Example: A test reveals that students struggle specifically with passive voice formation.

📈
Progress/Formative Test
Monitors learning DURING instruction
Click to expand

Purpose: Check ongoing progress and provide feedback for improvement.

When: Throughout the course at regular intervals.

Example: Weekly quizzes to check understanding of recent material.

🏆
Achievement Test
Measures what was TAUGHT in a specific course
Click to expand

Purpose: Evaluate how well learners mastered the specific content taught.

When: At the END of a unit, term, or course.

Example: End-of-unit test covering the grammar and vocabulary from Unit 5.

Key: Tests what was TAUGHT (course content) — "test what you taught."

🌍
Proficiency Test
Measures OVERALL ability regardless of specific course
Click to expand

Purpose: Measure overall language ability for real-world use.

When: Independent of any specific course; for certification.

Examples: TOEFL, IELTS, Cambridge exams (FCE, CAE, CPE).

Key: NOT tied to specific course content — measures general proficiency.

💡Key Distinction: ACHIEVEMENT tests measure what was TAUGHT (course-specific). PROFICIENCY tests measure overall ability (not tied to any course).
Section 4 · Principles of Testing

Key Principles: Validity, Reliability, Practicality, Washback

✅
What Makes a Good Test?
A good test must be VALID (measures what it claims), RELIABLE (consistent results), PRACTICAL (feasible to administer), and have positive WASHBACK (beneficial impact on teaching).

Validity — "Does it measure what it claims to measure?"

Definition: The extent to which a test measures what it is supposed to measure
Key principle: "Test what you taught" — test must align with learning objectives
Example: A speaking test should assess speaking ability, not writing or grammar knowledge

Types of Validity

Content validity — Test covers the content that was taught
Face validity — Test APPEARS to measure what it should (perception)
Construct validity — Test measures the underlying skill/ability (e.g., "reading comprehension")
Criterion validity — Test results correlate with external measures

Reliability — "Does it give consistent results?"

Definition: The consistency and stability of test results across different administrations
A reliable test: Same students get similar scores when tested again under same conditions
Threats: Ambiguous questions, inconsistent scoring, test conditions, time of day

Types of Reliability

Test-retest reliability — Same test, same students, different time → similar results
Inter-rater reliability — Different scorers give same scores (for subjective tests)
Internal consistency — All items measure the same construct
⚠️Exam Scenario: If students score very differently on the same test given twice, the test LACKS RELIABILITY.

Practicality — "Is it feasible to administer?"

Definition: The feasibility of administering and scoring within available resources
Considerations: Time, cost, ease of administration, scoring ease, resources needed
A practical test: Easy to administer, score, and interpret within constraints
Example problem: A 3-hour oral interview for 200 students lacks practicality

Washback (Backwash) Effect — "How does it influence teaching?"

Definition: The impact of a test on teaching and learning
Positive washback: Test encourages good teaching practices and meaningful learning
Negative washback: Test leads to "teaching to the test," ignoring important skills
Example of negative: If exams only test grammar, teachers neglect speaking → bad washback
💡Morocco context: If the Bac only tests reading/writing, teachers may neglect speaking/listening. This is NEGATIVE WASHBACK.
Section 5 · Test Item Types

Test Item Formats — Objective vs Subjective

📝
Choosing the Right Test Format
Test items can be OBJECTIVE (one correct answer, easy to score) or SUBJECTIVE (open-ended, require judgment to score).
FeatureObjective ItemsSubjective Items
AnswersOne correct answerMultiple possible responses
ScoringEasy, consistent, quickRequires judgment; may vary
ReliabilityHigh (no scorer variation)Lower (depends on scorer)
ExamplesMCQ, T/F, matching, fill-inEssays, compositions, oral
TestsRecognition, discrete itemsProduction, integrated skills

Objective Test Items

Multiple Choice (MCQ) — Choose the correct answer from options
True/False — Indicate if statements are true or false
Matching — Match items from two columns
Fill-in-the-blank / Cloze — Complete gaps with correct words
Ordering/Sequencing — Put items in correct order

Advantages: High reliability, quick scoring, covers more content. Disadvantages: Tests recognition not production; guessing possible.

Subjective Test Items

Essays/Compositions — Extended writing on a topic
Short-answer questions — Brief written responses
Oral interviews — Face-to-face speaking assessment
Presentations — Formal spoken performance

Advantages: Tests production, creativity, integrated skills. Disadvantages: Scoring is subjective; time-consuming; lower reliability.

💡Improving reliability: Use RUBRICS with clear criteria and scoring guides to make subjective scoring more consistent.

Performance-Based Assessment

Definition: Learners demonstrate knowledge through real-world tasks
Examples: Role-plays, presentations, projects, portfolios
Authentic assessment: Tasks mirror real-life language use
Aligns with: CLT, CBLT, Task-Based Learning

Moroccan exam note: Performance assessment = open-ended tasks like "describing a picture," "public speaking," NOT multiple choice.

Section 6 · Scoring Methods

Scoring Methods — Holistic vs Analytic

For subjective assessments (essays, speaking), teachers need clear scoring criteria. Two main approaches are HOLISTIC and ANALYTIC scoring.

FeatureHolistic ScoringAnalytic Scoring
ApproachSingle overall score based on general impressionSeparate scores for different criteria
SpeedFasterSlower, more detailed
FeedbackLess specificDetailed feedback on each criterion
Example"This essay is a 7 out of 10""Content: 8, Organization: 6, Grammar: 7, Vocabulary: 8"
Best forQuick assessment of large numbersFormative feedback, detailed evaluation

Holistic Scoring

One overall score based on the total impression
Pros: Faster, practical for large groups
Cons: Less detailed feedback; harder to identify specific weaknesses

Analytic Scoring

Separate scores for each criterion (content, grammar, vocabulary, organization, etc.)
Pros: Detailed feedback; identifies specific strengths/weaknesses
Cons: More time-consuming; requires well-defined rubric

Rubrics — Scoring Guides

Definition: A set of criteria and descriptions for different performance levels
Purpose: Make scoring more consistent (improve reliability)
Components: Criteria + performance level descriptors + scores
Benefit: Increases inter-rater reliability (different scorers give similar grades)
💡Key Point: Rubrics help improve RELIABILITY of subjective assessments by making scoring criteria clear and consistent.

Understanding Assessment & Testing
~60 seconds · British English

Formative assessment happens throughout the course — it's assessment FOR learning, helping teachers and students understand progress and make improvements. "Finally, good tests must be valid — they measure what they claim to measure. They must be reliable — giving consistent results. They should be practical — feasible to administer. "The washback effect refers to the impact that a test has on teaching and learning. Positive washback occurs when a test encourages good teaching practices and meaningful learning.
